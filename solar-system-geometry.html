Solar System Geometry

This is a to-scale model of the solar system showing all nine planetary orbits, the Sun, and Earth's Moon, computed from real JPL Keplerian orbital elements at the J2000 epoch with secular drift rates. Every orbit is drawn with its actual eccentricity — notice how nearly circular Venus is compared to the elongated ellipses of Mercury and Pluto. Kepler's equation is solved numerically for each body every frame. The Moon orbits at just 0.00257 AU from Earth, so zoom into the inner system to see it separate.

The gravity line system is what makes this tool unique. Below the viewport, each of the 10 bodies has its own column with 10 checkboxes — one for every other body it can be connected to, plus an "all" toggle at the bottom. Earth's column also includes the Moon. Check any pair and a solid line appears between them showing the real-time distance in AU. With all 46 possible connections drawn, you see the complete gravitational geometry of the solar system: every body pulling on every other body, the web of forces that holds it together. Animate forward in time and watch this geometric web breathe — triangles form and dissolve, distances stretch and compress, alignments come and go.

The time controls let you slide ±200 years from today, type a specific date, or animate forward at adjustable speed. The orbital element model is accurate within this range — beyond that, perturbation effects accumulate and the simple Keplerian model diverges from reality.

Scroll to zoom · Drag to pan · Use the view presets to jump between inner system, gas giant, and full system scales. Built for widescreen displays.
